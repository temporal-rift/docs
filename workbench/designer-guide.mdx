title: "Designer Guide"
description: "From a configured experiment to a reproduced case in the Simulation Workbench"

The Simulation Workbench web application takes a balance question through the whole research loop: configure an experiment, freeze it, run it, read the report, compare variants, inspect a case, and reproduce it. This guide walks through that loop in the order a designer follows it. The [Simulation Workbench API](/api/simulation) describes what the application does underneath.

You sign in with the workbench's identity provider. The application offers each action according to your scopes: reading needs `simulation:read`, changing anything needs `simulation:write`, and observer replay additionally needs `simulation:observe`. An action your session may not take is shown as unavailable and names the missing scope.

## 1. Configure and preview

On **Experiments → New experiment**, you can **Start from** a template: *Baseline policy comparison* (one variant; the tables *faction-greedy* and *random*, each playing one policy on every seat) or *Rules variant comparison* (two variants; the table *random*). A template fills one variant slot per variant it needs with the bundles the lanes serve, in order, and proposes seed `42`, player counts 3, 4 and 5, every faction set, and the execution bounds. Every value it fills stays editable, and your experiment name is kept.

Name the experiment, label each variant and choose its **Bundle** from the rules and content bundles the lanes serve. The bundle's artifacts and effective digests, and the service images and contract versions it pins, are shown read-only: you never type a digest. Variants can be compared only when their bundles pin the same services and contracts; otherwise the page names the fields that differ and the draft cannot be previewed or frozen. If no lane is configured, the page says so and nothing can be frozen. Then choose the policies (bot policy bundles from the catalog), then the **tables**: a table gives a name and one policy to each seat, so a table can mix policies, such as a random seat among faction-greedy seats. Every policy you choose must be used by a seat of some table, and a table names a policy for every seat up to your largest player count. Then choose the seeds, player counts and faction sets, and the execution bounds. A value the service refuses is marked on its own field, and an unsent draft survives a reload or an expired session.

**Preview matrix** shows the exact set of cases the experiment will run: the total, the count per variant, table and player count, and a page of case coordinates. The total is variants × tables × seeds × the games of one seed for your player counts and faction sets (55 for all three player counts and every faction set). Nothing is stored by a preview.

## 2. Freeze

**Freeze this experiment…** asks you to confirm the digest and case count you were shown. A frozen experiment never changes; **Change: new draft from this experiment** starts a new draft from it and leaves the original as it was.

## 3. Launch and follow a run

**Launch a run** starts the cases on the workbench's isolated lanes; each case plays only on a lane serving its variant's rules and content. If some variant has no such lane, the launch is refused, the refusal names the variant, and no run is created. The run page shows its state and the counts of requested, pending, running, succeeded, failed and cancelled cases, and refreshes while the run is open. A run can be cancelled until it settles and resumed only from `INTERRUPTED`. Failed cases are listed with their code and message and link to the case.

Cases that have not succeeded are never results. Anywhere data covers only part of the run, it is marked **Partial**.

## 4. Read the report

A report is organised in cohorts, each of one variant, one table and one player count. Each shows its eligible games and independent blocks and then every metric with its value, denominator and interval.

| You see | It means |
|---|---|
| Interval | A 95% interval over independent seed blocks |
| Insufficient sample | Fewer than two independent blocks support the value, so there is no interval |
| No data | The denominator is 0 |
| Unknown count | Opportunities whose playability could not be determined; they are excluded from the denominator |
| Partial report | Some requested cases have not succeeded |

**Export JSON** and **Export CSV** download exactly what the service returned.

### Judge a variant against the balance criterion

The **Evaluation** panel on the run page applies a balance criterion version to one variant of the run. Choose the variant, the criterion version and the mode: screening ranks and discards candidates and never confirms, confirmation may confirm. The panel is computed from the run's cases every time it is read.

| You see | It means |
|---|---|
| Rejected | A gate line fails: its whole interval lies outside its band |
| Confirmed | Confirmation mode, a satisfied sample, every pooled gate met and no scored line failing |
| Unconfirmed | Nothing rejects the variant and nothing confirms it yet |
| Invalid | The run cannot be used as evidence; the panel lists every reason and shows no score |
| Score | The weighted sum of squared deviations of the scored lines; lower is closer to the targets |
| Confirmation sample | The seeds the run has against the seeds the criterion requires, and whether the full case matrix was played |

The table lists every evaluated line with its table, cohort, value, interval, band, deviation and verdict (meets, fails or inconclusive) and can be filtered to failing or inconclusive lines. The panel always states that bot results say nothing about balance among human players.

Criterion version 2 adds a confirmation-only table, *random-seat-vs-faction-greedy-v1*, in which seat 0 plays at random and the other seats play faction-greedy. Its line **G-07** reads the random seat only: a faction's win rate on that seat, divided by the win reference of the whole table, must not exceed 1.0, so a faction that wins more often than an average seat when a random player holds it is flagged. Its confirmation sample is 104 seeds over the full case matrix.

## 5. Compare two variants

On **Comparisons**, choose the run and variant on each side. Cases are paired when they share seed, player count, table and seat assignment. A comparison lists the differences the two sides declare (rules or content), the matched blocks, the pairs left out because a side failed or has not finished, and each paired difference with its interval. If the two runs differ in anything beyond the declared variant, the comparison is refused and the differences are named.

## 6. Inspect a case

Open a case from its run to see its seats, every attempt with its state and failure, and its result, or the plain statement that it has none.

**Replay** shows one perspective at a time:

- **View as player** shows only the chosen seat's steps and only what that seat knew at each step: no opponent's hand or faction and no information it had not yet earned. Step numbers belong to the whole case, so a seat's steps skip the numbers of other seats. Use **Later steps** and **Earlier steps** to page.
- **View as observer…** requires `simulation:observe`. It is labelled **Observer evidence** and shows every seat's steps followed by the raw events, one list per source in the order that source delivered them. Without the scope, access is denied and nothing is shown. Observer evidence is for inspection only and is never used by bot policies.

Switching seat or perspective discards the previous view's data before anything else is shown.

## 7. Reproduce a case

**Reproduce this case** replays the saved case and compares it with the recording. It checks the case; it never adds a research sample and never changes the case's result.

| You see | It means |
|---|---|
| Waiting | The lane that played the case is busy or serves other rules or content. The reproduction starts when it is free and serving this case's variant again; this is not a failure. |
| Running | The case is being replayed. |
| Match | Every accepted decision and the final result equal the recording. |
| Diverged | The first difference: its step, its kind, and the expected and actual values. |
| The reproduction failed | It could not be completed this time. This says nothing about the case. |
| Cannot be reproduced | The case has no saved result or transcript, or its pinned configuration no longer matches. |

A divergence is one of `OBSERVATION`, `DECISION`, `WINDOW`, `MISSING_STEP`, `UNEXPECTED_STEP`, `UNEXPECTED_WINDOW` or `COMMAND_REJECTED`, or, after the last step, `END_REASON`, `WINNERS`, `FINAL_SCORES`, `ERAS` or `SEMANTIC_DIGEST`.

## 8. Check that the simulator plays the real game

The configuration search plays on an in-process simulator. A fidelity check compares it with the real services. Run one when you judge that a change could make the simulator play a different game; nothing waits for one. The **Simulator fidelity** page shows whether a check has passed for the simulator you are running, and lets you start one.

**The simulator's source revisions** lists the revision of each of the three services the simulator embeds. The page says either that the simulator is *established*, because a passing check exists for exactly these revisions, or that it is *not established*, because none does. A passing check applies only to the revisions it ran with, so the simulator is not established again whenever it embeds a new revision, until a check passes on it. This is information only: campaigns and confirmations play either way.

**Start a check** takes a seed and, on **Start a check**, plays the full set of cases on the simulator for that seed: three, four and five players, every faction set and seat rotation, both uniform tables (faction-greedy and random), for the baseline and for two variants that set as many parameters as the constraints allow at the minimum and at the maximum of their ranges. It then reproduces every case on one deployed lane. Starting is refused while another check is queued or running, and when the simulator is not enabled or no deployed lane matches its source revisions, content, contract versions and bot identities.

Open a check to follow it. It shows its state, the simulator run that plays its cases (linked once the check begins), the case counts, the builds of the simulator and of the deployed lane, the variants with their overrides, and the cases, which you can filter by variant and by result.

The counts stay at zero while the simulator plays, because the check has cases only once its run completes; follow that phase on the run's page. To abandon a check, cancel its run there: the check then fails with `RUN_CANCELLED`, and a new one can be started.

| Result of a case | It means |
|---|---|
| Pending | Not reproduced yet. |
| Match | The deployed lane played the case exactly as the simulator did. |
| Diverged | The deployed lane ran the case to its end and differed. The case shows the step and the kind of the first difference. |
| Cannot be reproduced | The simulator could not play the case, or the reproduction could not reach an ending. It says nothing about fidelity, but the check does not pass. |

A check **passes** only when it has at least one case and every case matches. One diverged case, or one case that cannot be reproduced, fails it; no difference is tolerated. A diverged case points at an in-memory behaviour of the simulator that differs from its production counterpart, and is a defect to fix before the simulator is trusted again.

**Download report** saves the check as a markdown report, named for the seed and the game-service revision, with the counts, both environments, the variants and the cases that did not match. A passing report is kept in the workbench repository, under `docs/fidelity`, as the evidence that the simulator was established for those revisions.

## 9. Search for a configuration

The **Campaigns** page lists the campaigns, newest first, with their state, round and the active time they have spent against their budget, and starts a new one. A campaign searches the rules of a baseline for configurations that meet the balance criterion: choose the baseline rules and content, the player counts, a budget in seconds of active time, how many games to play at once, two different master seeds, and the size of the search (candidates in the first round, seeds per candidate, how many candidates each round keeps, and how long the short list is). The campaign plays the tables of the balance criterion, so you do not choose policies or seats: the search plays the criterion's screening tables and the confirmation plays its confirmation tables. The form checks what it can before sending anything, and shows a refusal of the service on the field it names.

A campaign starts by itself, as soon as the workbench picks it up.

The campaign page shows the state, the budget spent and remaining, the round, the short list and the **Decision log**, which records every candidate generated, kept, discarded or short-listed, with the reason and the evidence for each. **Pause campaign** stops the budget and starts no new game, and games already playing finish; **Resume campaign** continues it; **Cancel campaign** ends it and keeps the log. **Games at once** changes how many games are played together from the next game on. The page refreshes while the campaign is open. When the search is complete, **Confirm short list** plays each short-listed candidate on fresh seeds on the simulator, one candidate after another and without a run for the baseline. At criterion version 2 it takes about 3.2 hours per candidate. It is refused when the simulator is not available, when no bundle serves a policy of the confirmation tables, or when a seed was already played by the search; the refusal names the reason. The **Confirmation** report shows, for each candidate, the progress of its run while it plays, then the verdict, the verdict of every criterion line and the confirmation sample, with the reminder that bot results say nothing about balance among human players.

## When something goes wrong

Every refusal is shown as a plain sentence with a way to continue. If the service cannot be reached, the last data stays on screen marked stale with a **Retry** button and nothing you entered is lost. Progress, errors and denials are announced to screen readers, and every control can be used from the keyboard.
