The Simulation Workbench web application takes a balance question through the whole research loop: configure an experiment, freeze it, run it, read the report, compare variants, inspect a case, and reproduce it. This guide walks through that loop in the order a designer follows it. The [Simulation Workbench API](/api/simulation) describes what the application does underneath.

You sign in with the workbench's identity provider. The application offers each action according to your scopes: reading needs `simulation:read`, changing anything needs `simulation:write`, and observer replay additionally needs `simulation:observe`. An action your session may not take is shown as unavailable and names the missing scope.

## 1. Configure and preview

On **Experiments → New experiment**, you can **Start from** a template: *Baseline policy comparison* (one variant; the random and faction-greedy policies, each on every seat) or *Rules variant comparison* (two variants; the random policy on every seat). A template fills one variant slot per variant it needs with the bundles the lanes serve, in order, and proposes seed `42`, player counts 3, 4 and 5, every faction set, and the execution bounds. Every value it fills stays editable, and your experiment name is kept.

Name the experiment, label each variant and choose its **Bundle** from the rules and content bundles the lanes serve. The bundle's artifacts and effective digests, and the service images and contract versions it pins, are shown read-only: you never type a digest. Variants can be compared only when their bundles pin the same services and contracts; otherwise the page names the fields that differ and the draft cannot be previewed or frozen. If no lane is configured, the page says so and nothing can be frozen. Then choose the bot policies (from the catalog) with the seats each one plays, the seeds, player counts and faction sets, and the execution bounds. A value the service refuses is marked on its own field, and an unsent draft survives a reload or an expired session.

**Preview matrix** shows the exact set of cases the experiment will run: the total, the count per variant and player count, and a page of case coordinates. Nothing is stored by a preview.

## 2. Freeze

**Freeze this experiment…** asks you to confirm the digest and case count you were shown. A frozen experiment never changes; **Change: new draft from this experiment** starts a new draft from it and leaves the original as it was.

## 3. Launch and follow a run

**Launch a run** starts the cases on the workbench's isolated lanes; each case plays only on a lane serving its variant's rules and content. If some variant has no such lane, the launch is refused, the refusal names the variant, and no run is created. The run page shows its state and the counts of requested, pending, running, succeeded, failed and cancelled cases, and refreshes while the run is open. A run can be cancelled until it settles and resumed only from `INTERRUPTED`. Failed cases are listed with their code and message and link to the case.

Cases that have not succeeded are never results. Anywhere data covers only part of the run, it is marked **Partial**.

## 4. Read the report

A report is organised in cohorts. Each shows its eligible games and independent blocks and then every metric with its value, denominator and interval.

| You see | It means |
|---|---|
| Interval | A 95% interval over independent seed blocks |
| Insufficient sample | Fewer than two independent blocks support the value, so there is no interval |
| No data | The denominator is 0 |
| Unknown count | Opportunities whose playability could not be determined; they are excluded from the denominator |
| Partial report | Some requested cases have not succeeded |

**Export JSON** and **Export CSV** download exactly what the service returned.

### Judge a variant against the balance criterion

The **Evaluation** panel on the run page applies a balance criterion version to one variant of the run. Choose the variant, the criterion version and the mode: screening ranks and discards candidates and never confirms, confirmation may confirm. The panel is computed from the run's cases every time it is read.

| You see | It means |
|---|---|
| Rejected | A gate line fails: its whole interval lies outside its band |
| Confirmed | Confirmation mode, a satisfied sample, every pooled gate met and no scored line failing |
| Unconfirmed | Nothing rejects the variant and nothing confirms it yet |
| Invalid | The run cannot be used as evidence; the panel lists every reason and shows no score |
| Score | The weighted sum of squared deviations of the scored lines; lower is closer to the targets |
| Confirmation sample | The seeds the run has against the seeds the criterion requires, and whether the full case matrix was played |

The table lists every evaluated line with its cohort, value, interval, band, deviation and verdict (meets, fails or inconclusive) and can be filtered to failing or inconclusive lines. The panel always states that bot results say nothing about balance among human players.

## 5. Compare two variants

On **Comparisons**, choose the run and variant on each side. A comparison lists the differences the two sides declare (rules or content), the matched blocks, the pairs left out because a side failed or has not finished, and each paired difference with its interval. If the two runs differ in anything beyond the declared variant, the comparison is refused and the differences are named.

## 6. Inspect a case

Open a case from its run to see its seats, every attempt with its state and failure, and its result, or the plain statement that it has none.

**Replay** shows one perspective at a time:

- **View as player** shows only the chosen seat's steps and only what that seat knew at each step: no opponent's hand or faction and no information it had not yet earned. Step numbers belong to the whole case, so a seat's steps skip the numbers of other seats. Use **Later steps** and **Earlier steps** to page.
- **View as observer…** requires `simulation:observe`. It is labelled **Observer evidence** and shows every seat's steps followed by the raw events, one list per source in the order that source delivered them. Without the scope, access is denied and nothing is shown. Observer evidence is for inspection only and is never used by bot policies.

Switching seat or perspective discards the previous view's data before anything else is shown.

## 7. Reproduce a case

**Reproduce this case** replays the saved case and compares it with the recording. It checks the case; it never adds a research sample and never changes the case's result.

| You see | It means |
|---|---|
| Waiting | The lane that played the case is busy or serves other rules or content. The reproduction starts when it is free and serving this case's variant again; this is not a failure. |
| Running | The case is being replayed. |
| Match | Every accepted decision and the final result equal the recording. |
| Diverged | The first difference: its step, its kind, and the expected and actual values. |
| The reproduction failed | It could not be completed this time. This says nothing about the case. |
| Cannot be reproduced | The case has no saved result or transcript, or its pinned configuration no longer matches. |

A divergence is one of `OBSERVATION`, `DECISION`, `WINDOW`, `MISSING_STEP`, `UNEXPECTED_STEP`, `UNEXPECTED_WINDOW` or `COMMAND_REJECTED`, or, after the last step, `END_REASON`, `WINNERS`, `FINAL_SCORES`, `ERAS` or `SEMANTIC_DIGEST`.

## 8. Check that the simulator plays the real game

The configuration search plays on an in-process simulator, so its results hold only if the simulator plays the same game as the real services. The **Simulator fidelity** page shows whether that has been checked for the simulator you are running, and lets you check it.

**The simulator's source revisions** lists the revision of each of the three services the simulator embeds. The page says either that the simulator is *established*, because a passing check exists for exactly these revisions, or that it is *not established*, because none does. A passing check applies only to the revisions it ran with, so the simulator is not established again whenever it embeds a new revision, until a check passes on it. A search campaign is refused while the simulator is not established.

**Start a check** takes a seed and, on **Start a check**, plays the full set of cases on the simulator for that seed: three, four and five players, every faction set and seat rotation, both baseline policies, for the baseline and for two variants that set as many parameters as the constraints allow at the minimum and at the maximum of their ranges. It then reproduces every case on one real lane. Starting is refused while another check is queued or running, and when the simulator is not enabled or no real lane matches its source revisions, content, contract versions and bot identities.

Open a check to follow it. It shows its state, the simulator run that plays its cases (linked once the check begins), the case counts, the builds of the simulator and of the real lane, the variants with their overrides, and the cases, which you can filter by variant and by result.

The counts stay at zero while the simulator plays, because the check has cases only once its run completes; follow that phase on the run's page. To abandon a check, cancel its run there: the check then fails with `RUN_CANCELLED`, and a new one can be started.

| Result of a case | It means |
|---|---|
| Pending | Not reproduced yet. |
| Match | The real lane played the case exactly as the simulator did. |
| Diverged | The real lane ran the case to its end and differed. The case shows the step and the kind of the first difference. |
| Cannot be reproduced | The simulator could not play the case, or the reproduction could not reach an ending. It says nothing about fidelity, but the check does not pass. |

A check **passes** only when it has at least one case and every case matches. One diverged case, or one case that cannot be reproduced, fails it; no difference is tolerated. A diverged case points at an in-memory behaviour of the simulator that differs from its production counterpart, and is a defect to fix before the simulator is trusted again.

**Download report** saves the check as a markdown report, named for the seed and the game-service revision, with the counts, both environments, the variants and the cases that did not match. A passing report is kept in the workbench repository, under `docs/fidelity`, as the evidence that the simulator was established for those revisions.

## When something goes wrong

Every refusal is shown as a plain sentence with a way to continue. If the service cannot be reached, the last data stays on screen marked stale with a **Retry** button and nothing you entered is lost. Progress, errors and denials are announced to screen readers, and every control can be used from the keyboard.
