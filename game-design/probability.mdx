---
title: "Probability Model"
description: "How outcome probabilities work, visibility tiers, and shift mechanics"
---


Each event has three possible outcomes. At the start of an era, they are distributed evenly: two at 33% and one at 34%. From there, players spend their action rounds trying to shift those numbers in their favour.

Three constraints hold throughout:

- The floor is **0%** and the ceiling is **90%** — no outcome can be guaranteed
- All three outcomes always sum to **100%**; shifting one redistributes the remainder proportionally across the other two
- Every probability is the outcome's **real chance of winning**, including after an outcome is erased
- Exact probabilities are **hidden by default** — only a player who played `SCAN` on that event can see them

## Banded probability reveal

After Action Round 2 closes, **probability bands** are publicly revealed:

| Band | Range |
|---|---|
| LOW | 0–30% |
| MEDIUM | 31–60% |
| HIGH | 61–90% |

Bands reveal the *results* of Round 2, not in-progress plays. Exact state remains private — Scan card only.

This creates a deliberate two-tier visibility model:

- **Band** — public, post-Round-2, available to all players
- **Exact** — private, Scan card only

Bands communicate directional momentum; exact values remain a strategic advantage for players who use Scan.

## Example shift

Starting state: A=40%, B=35%, C=25%

Player plays `PUSH` targeting outcome A (+20%):
- A goes from 40% → 60% (+20)
- B and C lose proportionally: B → 35 - 20×(35/60) ≈ 23%, C → 25 - 20×(25/60) ≈ 17%
- Result: A=60%, B=23%, C=17% (sums to 100%)

## How the winning outcome is chosen

At resolution, each event's winning outcome is drawn at random, weighted by its final probability. An outcome at
60% is more likely to win than one at 40% — but it isn't guaranteed. This is why the ceiling matters: a probability
can climb as high as 90%, but the outcome it favours can still lose.

Tied outcomes are equally likely to win. A tie is a **Dead Heat** paradox only when a Collide forced it this era.

## Erasing an outcome

`ANNIHILATE` drops an outcome to **0%** for the rest of the era, and it can't win. Its probability moves to the
event's other outcomes in proportion to their current values, so they keep their relative odds and together hold the
full 100%. Starting from A=33%, B=33%, C=34%, erasing C gives A=50%, B=50%, C=0%.

- Redistribution never pushes an outcome past the 90% ceiling. At A=85%, B=5%, C=10%, erasing C gives A=90%, B=10%.
- A sealed outcome keeps its probability, so only the unsealed survivor takes the erased weight. Erasing a sealed
  outcome removes its seal.
- Later shifts never move weight into or out of an erased outcome. A card that targets it, swings from or to it, or
  collides it has no effect.
- An erasure is rejected, and costs nothing, when the survivors can't hold the erased weight within 0–90%. Erasing an
  event's last outcome, or erasing down to a single outcome that would win with certainty, is always rejected.
- Erasures on the same event in the same round are judged together: if they can't all fit, none of them apply.

A cascaded or stalled event keeps its probabilities, so an outcome erased last era comes back at 0% and can be pushed
up again. A `CASCADE` erases it again for one more era instead.

## Scan card

`SCAN` reveals the exact probability state of one event to the player who played it, and **keeps it current for the
rest of the era**. You see updated values at the close of every remaining round, while everyone else waits for bands
after Round 2. Each reading is stamped with the round it was taken, so you can tell how fresh it is.

The reveal is private and is never broadcast — no other player learns you scanned, or what you saw. It does not grant
future-era access, even when a cascaded event returns with its probability state intact.

The `SCAN` grade decides how many events one play covers: **I** one event, **II** two, **III** all three.

Scan cannot be played in Round 3 — the era resolves immediately afterwards, so there would be no action left to use
what you learned.
